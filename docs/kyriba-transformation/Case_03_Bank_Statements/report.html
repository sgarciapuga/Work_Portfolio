Kyriba transformation · case study 03

How prior-day bank statements moved from bank portal downloads and SharePoint files to automatic delivery into Kyriba, where they are mapped, reconciled and turned into one daily cash position.

NoteEvidence boundary

This case study is based on the supplied Case 03 - Bank Statements presentation. The statement flow, reconciliation rules and impact figures below are reported as described in that source. Banks are anonymised as Bank A and Bank B, as in the source, and the statement aggregator is not named. The case does not add client, bank or aggregator names, implementation dates, or independently audited benefits.

How bank statements reach Treasury

Every morning, the previous day’s bank statements are loaded into Kyriba.

  1. Bank B, direct. Bank B is connected directly to Kyriba through EBICS and sends camt.053 statements.
  2. Bank A, through an aggregator. Bank A sends MT940 statements through an external bank statement aggregator.
  3. Other banks, through the same aggregator. About 20 other banks send MT940 statements through the same aggregator, covering about 100 group entities in many currencies.
  4. About 300 accounts. In total, about 300 bank accounts report into Kyriba.
  5. Same core banks as Case 02. Bank A and Bank B are the same core banks as in Case 02: Payments Connectivity, now fully connected for both payments and statements.

All statements are prior-day. They arrive in the morning and are loaded into Kyriba early, ready for the daily cash position. Before this project, statements came from bank portal downloads or SFTP files in SharePoint.

camt.053 is the ISO 20022 XML bank-to-customer end-of-day statement; MT940 is the equivalent SWIFT MT format. EBICS is a file-transfer protocol used for direct bank–corporate connectivity.

From bank portals to automatic statements

Before: manual process After: statement connectivity
Many statements were downloaded from bank portals. Prior-day statements arrive automatically every morning.
Some statements were delivered to SharePoint through SFTP. Bank B sends directly through EBICS (camt.053); Bank A (MT940) and about 20 other banks send through an external aggregator.
The legacy TMS calculated the cash balance from its ledger, built from all financial transactions (FX, ETD, OTC derivatives, CDS, fixed income, netting, intercompany, invoices, collateral). Statements are loaded into Kyriba early in the morning by AUTOINTEG, with balances and transactions.
Some deals, such as collateral movements and invoices, were entered by hand as journal vouchers. BTC mapping gives each statement line the correct flow code.
Reconciliation was a mix: by hand, or in Power BI with Dataflows connected to SharePoint. Reconciliation rules (AUTOREC) match lines with the forecasts in Kyriba, or create the cash flow when there is no forecast. Lines with no match go on hold for manual reconciliation.
— Today’s expected cash position shows what to invest or divest.

AUTOINTEG and AUTOREC are the names the source uses for the automatic statement integration and reconciliation tasks. A BTC is a bank transaction code, the code the bank puts on each statement line.

The statement flow

The redesigned flow follows five steps: banks send → AUTOINTEG loads → BTC to flow code → AUTOREC → cash position.

Step What happens
1. Banks send Bank B sends camt.053 statements directly through EBICS. Bank A and about 20 other banks send MT940 statements through an external aggregator.
2. AUTOINTEG loads Statements are loaded into the Cash & Liquidity module early each morning, with balances and transactions.
3. BTC to flow code Each bank transaction code is mapped to a flow code: credit and debit transfers, fees, dividends, miscellaneous.
4. AUTOREC Statement lines are matched with the forecasts already in Kyriba (from netting, collateral and fees), or the cash flow is created when there is no forecast. Lines with no match go on hold for manual reconciliation.
5. Cash position The cash position worksheet shows today’s expected position. Treasury uses it each morning to see what to invest or divest. The planned next step is for group entities to reconcile in Kyriba, not in Excel.

How reconciliation works

AUTOREC matches statement lines with the forecasts Treasury already has in Kyriba:

# Flow Statement line Matched with forecast from
1 Netting TRF with “netting” in the description Netting deal
2 Treasury transfers Transfer between Bank A and Bank B Treasury transfer (balancing transfer)
3 Collateral Collateral posted Financial transaction: collateral (variable balance)
4 RCF RCF principal or interest Financial transaction: RCF principal and interest
5 Fees Fees Intercompany fees
6 Invoices Payment to a supplier Third party invoice paid by Treasury
7 Money market funds MMF subscription or redemption Financial transaction: MMF deal (variable balance)

When there is no forecast, AUTOREC creates the cash flow. When there is no match, the line goes on hold for manual reconciliation. The plan is for other group entities to reconcile in Kyriba instead of in Excel or by hand.

The netting and treasury transfer forecasts are the same flows that Case 02 sends to the banks as payments, so the statements close the loop on those payments.

Daily statement checks

Treasury runs four checks every morning, all in Kyriba. Only after these checks does Treasury use the cash position to invest or divest.

Check Where to check If something is wrong
1. Did all statements arrive? Bank connectivity cockpit and bank connection status report An empty bank statement balance means the statement was not received. Contact the bank.
2. Were they integrated? Rejected transactions Codes 01 to 04: fix the set-up and reintegrate. Code 90: the previous day’s statement is missing; contact the bank.
3. Is anything on hold? On hold column in the bank/cash balance variance report Lines with no match. Do the manual reconciliation.
4. Do bank and cash agree? Bank connectivity cockpit and bank/cash balance variance report Bank ledger and flow ledger differ. Check pending items, duplicates, manual actuals or a missing initial balance.

Reported business impact

The presentation reports the following outcomes:

Operational risk: ~300 bank accounts

About 300 bank accounts report automatically. Previously, many statements were downloaded from bank portals or sent to SharePoint by SFTP.

Efficiency: ~15 minutes every morning

Approximately 15 minutes saved every morning on reconciliation. Previously, reconciliation was done by hand, or in Power BI with Dataflows connected to SharePoint.

Control: 1 daily cash position

One daily cash position in Kyriba. Previously, the legacy TMS calculated the cash balance from its ledger of financial transactions, with some deals entered by hand.

The coverage figure spans 2 core banks (Bank A and Bank B) plus about 20 other banks through an external aggregator, covering about 100 group entities. The source does not give a monthly total for the time saving; on the same one-run-per-working-day basis used in Case 02 (about 21 a month), 15 minutes a morning would be roughly 5 hours a month. That conversion is added here for comparison only. All figures are source-reported estimates, not measured or audited savings.

Delivery contribution

The case demonstrates the Treasury-functional contribution to bank statement connectivity work:

  • mapping where every bank’s statements come from (direct EBICS for Bank B, an external aggregator for Bank A and about 20 other banks) and how they reach Kyriba each morning;
  • defining how bank transaction codes map to Kyriba flow codes;
  • designing reconciliation rules that match statement lines with the forecasts Treasury already holds for netting, treasury transfers, collateral, RCF, fees, invoices and money market funds;
  • setting out the four daily checks, where to run them in Kyriba and what to do when one fails; and
  • making the operational, efficiency and control benefits visible, alongside the planned roll-out of reconciliation in Kyriba to group entities.

Technical ownership of the bank and aggregator connections, statement formats and infrastructure is outside the evidence provided by the source presentation.
