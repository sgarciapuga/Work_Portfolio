Kyriba transformation · case study 02

How Treasury payments moved from netting-file downloads, bank portals and faxes to a single approval point in Kyriba with direct connections to the group’s two core banks.

NoteEvidence boundary

This case study is based on the supplied Case 02 - Payments Connectivity presentation. The payment flows and impact figures below are reported as described in that source. Banks are anonymised as Bank A and Bank B, as in the source. The case does not add client or bank names, implementation dates, connectivity channel details, or independently audited benefits.

How Treasury payments work

Treasury acts as the cash hub for the group. Before looking at what changed, it helps to see which payments Treasury generates and why most intercompany activity never reaches a bank.

  1. Treasury as cash hub. Group entities deposit surplus cash with Treasury, or draw it down as loans or deposit withdrawals.
  2. Intercompany activity. Group entities can also pay each other for intercompany fees, drawdowns or distributions. These transactions stay inside the treasury system; only the netted amounts go out as payments.
  3. One daily netting position. Treasury tracks what is coming in and going out across every entity. The netting process runs one day before the value date, and net movements generate netted payments to the right entities.
  4. Third parties and bank transfers. Treasury also makes third party payments and moves cash between the two banks where it holds accounts, Bank A and Bank B.
  5. One payment run. Netted payments, third party payments and treasury transfers are generated and sent for approval in one payment run, ready for cash to settle on the value date.

Before this project, these payments were sent using manual file uploads, bank portals and faxes.

How netting works

Transactions between group entities are settled inside Kyriba. Cash moves through a bank only when it enters or leaves Treasury. The source illustrates this with a chain in which Entity A pays Entity B and Entity B pays Entity C, under four funding scenarios:

Scenario Entity A’s funding Entity C’s surplus Real cash in Real cash out
1 Withdraws from its deposit with Treasury Deposited with Treasury None None
2 Withdraws from its deposit with Treasury Paid out to Entity C None Payment to Entity C’s bank
3 Entity A’s bank sends cash to Treasury Deposited with Treasury From Entity A’s bank None
4 Entity A’s bank sends cash to Treasury Paid out to Entity C From Entity A’s bank Payment to Entity C’s bank

In every scenario the intermediate payments (A to B, B to C) stay inside Kyriba. That is why the payment run only has to carry the netted amounts, plus third party payments and treasury transfers.

From manual files to direct bank connectivity

Before: manual process After: bank connectivity
Netting ran automatically, but the netting report was downloaded manually. The netting process is scheduled in Kyriba, and can also be triggered manually by the user.
The file was downloaded and re-uploaded to the legacy TMS, which sent payments to the bank. Netted payments are released from the Cash & Liquidity module straight to the Payments module.
Third party and other non-netted payments were instructed manually in the bank portal. Third party payments are instructed in Kyriba using templates in the Payments module.
Treasury transfers between Bank A and Bank B were instructed manually, through the portal or by fax and email to the Bank B team. Treasury transfers between Bank A and Bank B are instructed in the Cash & Liquidity module.
Payments were checked manually in the bank portal, then sent for approval. Payments are reviewed and sent for approval in Kyriba.
— Once approved, a pain.001 payment instruction goes to Bank A, or to Bank B for treasury transfers from Bank B to Bank A.
— Kyriba receives pain.002 status feedback from the bank, and the bank also emails feedback on the XML file.

pain.001 and pain.002 are the ISO 20022 XML messages for customer payment initiation and payment status reporting.

The integrated payments flow

The redesigned flow follows six steps: instruct → netting → release to Payments → approve → send to bank → bank pays.

Step What happens
1. Instruct Group entities record deposits, withdrawals and FX deals (Financial Transactions module) and intercompany fees, distributions and drawdowns. Treasury instructs treasury transfers and third party payments.
2. Netting The netting run in the Cash & Liquidity module produces the netted payments.
3. Release to Payments Netted payments and treasury transfers are released to the Payments module. Third party payments are instructed there directly, using templates.
4. Approve All payments are reviewed and approved in the Payments module.
5. Send to bank Kyriba sends a pain.001 payment instruction to the bank and receives pain.002 status feedback. All payments are initiated using Bank A; only transfers from Bank B to Bank A are initiated in Bank B.
6. Bank pays The bank pays group entities and third parties, and moves treasury transfers between Bank A and Bank B.

Reported business impact

The presentation reports the following outcomes:

Operational risk: 2 banks connected

Both core banks are connected straight-through. Previously, the netting file was downloaded, reloaded into the legacy TMS and sent through the bank portal.

Efficiency: ~6 hours saved per month

Approximately 15 minutes saved per payment run, and at least 5 minutes saved on each treasury transfer and third party payment. Transfers previously sent by fax save more.

Control: 1 place to approve

All payments are approved in one place. Previously, approvals were spread across the Bank A portal, fax to Bank B and email to the Bank B team.

The source bases the efficiency estimate on one payment run per working day (about 21 a month), 6 treasury transfers and 5 third party payments a month. Those assumptions are consistent with the headline figure: 21 runs × 15 minutes is about 5¼ hours, and 11 transfers and payments × at least 5 minutes is about 1 hour more. These are source-reported estimates, not measured or audited savings.

Delivery contribution

The case demonstrates the Treasury-functional contribution to bank connectivity work:

  • mapping the full payment population (netted payments, third party payments and treasury transfers) into a single payment run;
  • explaining how netting keeps intercompany settlement inside Kyriba so only net cash movements reach the banks;
  • defining which Kyriba module each payment type is instructed in, and where it is released, reviewed and approved;
  • clarifying which bank initiates which payments, including the Bank B to Bank A exception; and
  • making the operational, efficiency and control benefits visible with stated estimation assumptions.

Technical ownership of the bank connection, message mapping and infrastructure is outside the evidence provided by the source presentation.
