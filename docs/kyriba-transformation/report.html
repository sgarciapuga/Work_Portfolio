From Treasury requirements to an operable, connected platform

A Treasury functional case study: translating operational requirements into Kyriba design, controlled integration validation and continuous improvement.

Treasury systems case study

This case study shows how Treasury operational requirements were translated into Kyriba functional design, controlled validation, connected processes and continuous improvement.

NoteEvidence boundary

This report is based on the supplied Kyriba Transformation presentation. It describes functional design, requirements validation, configuration, administration and collaboration with IT, banks, custodians, vendors and Finance. It does not claim ownership of technical infrastructure, API keys, architecture, delivery dates or quantitative performance outcomes unless separately evidenced.

Role and contribution

My role sat between Treasury operations, Kyriba solution design and hands-on system administration. I translated Treasury requirements into functional specifications, reviewed consultant-designed solutions, tested workflows and mappings, and maintained post-go-live enhancements.

Functional design

Captured operational requirements, challenged pain points and mapped Treasury needs to Kyriba capabilities, rules and configuration behaviour.

Solution validation

Reviewed workflows, mappings, templates and outputs with implementation teams and business stakeholders to confirm that the solution worked from a Treasury-user perspective.

Ongoing stewardship

Maintained users, roles, permissions, system data and optimisation cycles, escalating technical components that required IT support.

Functional ownership Collaboration and boundary
Requirements, workflow design, configuration review, functional testing, Treasury-led administration and optimisation. Worked with IT, implementation teams, banks, providers, custodians and Finance on connectivity, specialist delivery and technical components.

Blueprinting & Functional Design

Start with Treasury’s operating reality

Functional design began with Treasury business roles, operational processes and pain points. Stakeholder conversations turned those needs into practical requirements that could be reviewed against Kyriba’s functional capabilities.

The scope included trade processing, banking and payments, FX integration, cash management, accounting, reporting, controls and approvals. The objective was not to treat Kyriba as an isolated application, but to make its behaviour workable for Treasury operations.

Requirements and alignment

Documented detailed operational requirements, challenged existing processes and identified pain points. Treasury needs were translated into functional specifications.

Workflow and controls

Designed end-to-end Treasury workflows, defined approval and control points, and identified opportunities to simplify manual activity.

Kyriba design and validation

Mapped requirements to Kyriba functionality, reviewed functional behaviour and configuration requirements, and supported functional and end-to-end testing.

TipPart takeaway

Treasury requirements were converted into practical Kyriba solutions while balancing operational needs, controls and system capabilities.

Core Data & Access Setup

Establish the operating foundation

The data foundation covered entities, banks, bank accounts and third parties. Kyriba-provided templates were completed and validated, relationships between core objects were established, and data structures were checked against Treasury operating requirements before system loading.

Configuration also covered main and system currencies, transaction types, product structures and product subscriptions aligned to business and user requirements.

Pair data quality with controlled access

The access model used role-based permissions, access boundaries, approval workflows and segregation-of-duties requirements. The source presentation gives Client User, Treasury User, Admin User and View-Only User as examples of user profiles.

Foundation area What the work established
Master data Entities, banks, bank accounts and third parties, with relationships between core data objects.

NoteScope note

Treasury-led administration describes functional responsibility for day-to-day Kyriba configuration and maintenance. It does not imply ownership of the underlying technical infrastructure.

Integration & Connectivity

Connect Kyriba to the wider Treasury ecosystem

This work covered Banking & Payments, Trade Management, ERP / Accounting and the Data / API Layer around Kyriba. The contribution focused on defining requirements, configuring or reviewing templates, coordinating with external parties, and validating outputs and exceptions.

The diagram shows the integration domains addressed in the transformation. It does not imply undocumented interface direction or technical ownership.

Integration inventory

Domain Functional focus Validation focus
Banking & Payments camt.053 statements, pain.001 / pain.002 payments, BTC mappings and reconciliation rules. Statement delivery, formats, transaction information, payment responses, statuses, matching behaviour and breaks.
Trade Management Money Market Funds platform, internal manual trade entry, custodian-bank post-trade processing and FX platform connectivity. Trade capture, transaction-specific templates, end-to-end processing, exceptions and final solution behaviour.
ERP / Accounting Financial accounting, accruals, mark-to-market (MTM), general-ledger (GL) generation and approval, cash accounting and ERP connectivity. Transaction templates, mappings, procedures, GL review and approval, and Data Platform output.
Data / API Layer Standard Kyriba Data API, Open API financial transactions, market data including FX rates, forwards, interest-rate curves and MMF rates. Required data fields, sources, outputs, gaps and documented data requirements.

Controlled connectivity in practice

For banking and payments, the work included connectivity setup support, statement and payment testing, BTC mapping, reconciliation rules and investigation of breaks in the Bank Connectivity Cockpit. Trade-management activity covered manual-entry requirements, template configuration, executed-trade analysis, provider/custodian coordination and exception testing.

For accounting, cash and financial transaction templates were reviewed and improved with Finance and the Kyriba team. The process from accruals and MTM through GL generation, review and approval was documented. In parallel, Open API requirements were defined with IT and outputs were validated through the Data Platform.

TipPart takeaway

The integration work connected Kyriba with the wider Treasury and financial ecosystem through functional requirements, controlled testing and exception review.

Optimisation & Continuous Improvement

Improve the implemented platform after go-live

After implementation, the focus shifted to visibility, usability and repeatable processes. This included personalised dashboards, tailored views and templates, simplified navigation, shortcuts and transaction input screens shaped around user roles and transaction needs.

User experience

Personalised dashboards gave clients a clearer view of positions and key Treasury information. Tailored views and templates supported the monitoring of new, historical and outstanding trades.

Easier operation

Menu structures and shortcuts made frequently used functions easier to access. Transaction input screens were personalised by transaction type and user requirements.

Repeatable delivery

Reporting requirements and delivery processes were designed for trades, positions, balances and interest. Trade-confirmation workflows used CSV output and email-only recipients to distribute confirmations to fund administrators.
